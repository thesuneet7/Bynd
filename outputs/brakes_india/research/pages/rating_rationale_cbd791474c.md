| Detailed Rationale<br>Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its ‘Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+’ ratings on the bank loan facilities of Brakes India Pvt Ltd (Brakes India).<br>The ratings continue to reflect the company’s leading position in the braking systems market, diversified revenue profile, steady operating efficiency and efficient working capital management, healthy financial risk profile and strong liquidity. These strengths are partially offset by high, though moderating, exposure to the passenger vehicle (PV) and cyclical medium and heavy commercial vehicle (M&HCV) segments, in addition to its relatively high dependence on Europe for exports.<br>The revenue remained flattish at Rs 7,509 crore in fiscal 2025 (Rs 7,489 crore in fiscal 2024), impacted by moderate offtake from the domestic automotive original equipment manufacturers (OEMs), and fall in exports in the foundry segment, which was offset by the healthy demand from the aftermarket segment. Broadly, Brakes India derives about 95% of its revenue from its Indian operations, while the remaining is contributed by its subsidiary in Oman, Dunes Oman LLC (FZC). Within the domestic business, the brakes division contributes about 75% of the revenue, which grew 2% on the back of the aftermarket segment. The foundry division, contributing the remaining revenue, fell by 18% in fiscal 2025 on account of fall in exports owing to end of life of some export contracts as well as a contraction in offtake from certain customers. Domestic segment revenue declined due to pre-agreed price reductions against input price decreases during the fiscal. Brakes India is well-positioned to register steady revenue growth, supported by its dominant market position in the domestic braking systems segment, diversified product portfolio, exit of global peers from traditional braking business, healthy segmental diversity, and strong clientele. Besides, growth in aftermarket and export is expected to support overall revenue growth of 3-5% over the medium term. <br>During June 2024, Brakes India partnered with Advics Co Ltd, Japan (Advics), and had established a joint venture (BIADS India Pvt Ltd), of which Brakes India shall be holding 51% and the rest will be held by Advics. This Joint Venture (JV), which is being set up at Shoologiri, near Hosur, TamilNadu, would manufacture certain advanced braking components for sale to the Indian OEMs from the last quarter of fiscal 2027.The initial capital outlay (phase I) for the greenfield project is estimated at Rs 375 crore, with an additional expenditure of Rs 130 crore anticipated in the second phase. The funding for the phase I project will be in the form of debt and equity (30:70) while the equity contribution shall be from the JV partners proportionate to their shareholding.<br>Operating profitability fell 170 basis points to 13% in fiscal 2025 from 14.7% in fiscal 2024 on account of decline in high margin exports across the foundry division and lower-than-expected revenue in the brakes division impacting the operating leverage; however, this was partially offset by the stable raw material costs during the period and improved profitability at Dunes Oman. The brakes division (~75% of consolidated revenue in fiscal 2024) is expected to register stable profitability while the foundry division profitability will moderate, though remain healthy, due to decline in the share of exports. The earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation (Ebitda) margin is expected to stabilise at 12-12.5% over the medium term on account of moderate demand expected from the domestic OEMs and expected increase in freight costs for exports, besides the initial gestational losses at BIADS. <br>Brakes India’s solid financial position continues to benefit from strong annual cash generation and minimal debt on its balance sheet relative to its strong networth of over ~Rs 3,000 crore as on March 31, 2025. Debt protection metrics, such as interest coverage, debt to EBITDA and total outside liabilities to tangible networth ratio, remained robust at 23.2 times, 0.3 time, and 0.46 time, respectively, as on March 31, 2025. The company had completely prepaid its long-term debt during fiscal 2025 by utilising its surpluses and cash accrual, leading to decline in total debt to Rs 289 crore from Rs 707 crore. <br>Brakes India had spent Rs 348 crore towards capital expenditure (capex), including for expansion of foundry capacity, and regular maintenance-related capex. For the fiscal 2026, overall annual capital spending is expected to increase to around Rs 500crore, which will be spenttowards related research and development, additional infrastructural capex to meet future needs besides the regular maintenance capex. Cash generation from existing businesses would be adequate for funding the capex activities. Brakes India also had liquid surplus of Rs 239 crore as of March 2025, thereby solidifying its liquidity. | |

| Key Rating Drivers & Detailed Description<br>Strengths:<br>- Leading position in the braking systems business: The group is one of the largest market leaders in the braking systems business, with a leading share in the market for domestic OEMs, including leadership in the commercial vehicle (CV) and tractor segments. While ZF Friedrichshafen AG (ZF) sold its ~50% stake in Brakes India in 2021 to other entities in the TVS group, the company continues to have technical and product licences from ZF. Brakes India has ongoing technology agreements for braking products with alternate technology partners. <br>Brakes India has also entered into a technology sharing agreement with Advics for development of advanced braking products. Advics was incorporated in 2001 as a joint venture between AISIN Corporation, Denso Corporation, and Sumitomo Electric Industries Ltd. Headquartered in Japan, Advics operates in markets worldwide, including America, China, ASEAN, and Europe, emerging as one of the leading players in the braking systems with advanced technologies.<br>Besides, the company will be investing in its own R&D (research and development), which will also enable development of new products that further strengthen its business risk profile. Also, some of the existing global players are vacating the traditional braking products space in India, and Brakes India has acquired some of these business, which will further support its revenue growth over the medium term.<br>- Diversified revenue profile reflecting strong relationships with domestic OEMs: The Brakes India group has diversified presence across the OEMs (60-65% of standalone revenue), aftermarket, and exports segments. It is also present across automobile segments, including CVs, passenger cars, utility vehicles, and tractors. Leading customers in the OEM segment include Tata Motors Ltd (Crisil AA+/Stable/Crisil A1+), Ashok Leyland Ltd, Maruti Suzuki India Ltd (Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+), and Mahindra and Mahindra Ltd (Crisil AAA/Stable/Crisil A1+).<br>While the group’s mainstay is braking components, increasing contribution from foundry operations enhance its product and geographical diversity. The company is also enhancing its presence in the aftermarket by introducing additional products such as gear oil, engine oil, rotors, slack adjusters and power steering fluid. Diversified revenue streams will ensure stability in performance over the medium term.<br>- Steady operating efficiency and efficient working capital management: Brakes India has an integrated manufacturing set-up, with its foundry division supplying around 35% of it’s output to of the brakes division. The company’s operations benefit from strong in-house design and development capabilities, enhanced by technical support from collaborators and state-of-the-art testing facilities. <br>The company has multi-locational facilities, which facilitates better inventory management and reduction in freight costs, besides enabling just-in-time (JIT) deliveries to OEMs. Due to its strong market position in the braking business, Brakes India has been able to improve margins by obtaining price revisions even during cyclical downturns. The company’s operating profitability has been 12-14% on a steady state basis, though the foundry division’s profitability is better. The Oman manufacturing unit benefits from lower cost of power, materials and freight, which helps the foundry division’s profitability as well, thereby improving operating profitability of the Oman unit to ~17% in fiscal 2025 from ~15% in fiscal 2024. Besides, the healthy operating efficiency is displayed by strong return on capital employed of ~27% times in fiscal 2025, which is expected to remain above 20% in fiscal 2026. <br>- Healthy financial risk profile and strong liquidity: Strong financial position continues to benefit from high annual cash generation and moderate debt on its balance sheet, thereby aiding robust debt metrics. Brakes India had prepaid its entire long-term debt during fiscal 2025, resulting in reduction in total debt to Rs 289 crore as on March 31, 2025, from Rs 770 crore earlier. This also improved gearing to 0.1 time from 0.3 time as on March 31, 2024. However, the prepayment of term debt was partially met through cash surpluses, which reduced to Rs 239 crore as of March 2025. <br>The annual capex is set to increase to around Rs 500-\\crore in the currentfiscals, which will be spent towards R&D, development of new products and specific infrastructure spend and replacement capital item expenditure. . This capex would be predominantly funded by internal accruals and may be partly by debt. However, despite the debt addition, the financial risk profile is expected to remain healthy with gearing below 0.5 time and net debt/Ebitda below 1 time.<br>Weaknesses:<br>- High exposure towards PV and the cyclical CV segments: Although the group’s clientele is spread across various segments in the automotive industry, it derives around 65% and ~30% of its revenue for the brakes division from PVs and CVs, respectively. The CV segment has displayed the highest cyclicality in the past decade; this, coupled with any prolonged slowdown in the PV segment, lends partial volatility to the group’s revenue.<br>- High dependence on Europe for exports: The group derived 18% of its total revenue from exports in the brakes and foundry divisions, which are expected to continue to fetch higher profitability compared to domestic sales. Among this, 70-75% of the revenue comes from Europe. However, the share of exports for Brakes India dropped to 18% in fiscal 2025 from 21% in fiscal 2024 and 25% in fiscal 2021 due to end of life of some export contracts as well as a contraction in offtake from certain customers. Any significant demand weakness from the European region will constrain the pace of growth in the export business. | |

| Liquidity: Strong<br>The liquidity is driven by expected cash accrual of more than Rs 650-700 crore over the medium term, and cash and equivalents of around Rs 239 crore as on March 31, 2025; which is expected to sufficiently fund annual capex of Rs 500-600 crore for this and next fiscals. Fund-based working capital limit of Rs 750 crore has been moderately utilised by over 30% (against sanctioned limit) in the 12 months through April 2025. Pursuant to its prepayment of long-term debt in fiscal 2025, Brakes India does not have any debt obligation until the next fiscal, after which debt obligation may arise for the project loan of Rs 120 crore to be availed at its subsidiary; however, this is expected to be comfortably serviced through internal accrual. | |

| Outlook:Stable<br>Crisil Ratings believes Brakes India will continue to benefit from established business risk profile in the braking systems and components business, strong relationship with several OEMs and other customers, diverse geographical presence, and adequate operating capability that will ensure steady business performance over the medium term. Besides, the company’s financial risk profile will remain healthy and gradually further improve, supported by strong annual cash generation amid its capital spending plans. | |

| Rating sensitivity factors<br>Upward factors:<br>- Better-than-expected revenue growth, including through strengthening of the market position in the brakes segment, and enhanced geographical diversity in revenue<br>- Sustenance of steady operating profitability leading to better-than-expected cash accrual (Rs 750-800 crore)<br>- Sustenance of healthy financial risk profile and debt metrics, as well as strong liquidity<br>Downward factors:<br>- Sluggish revenue growth and moderation in operating margin (below 8-9%) on a sustained basis due to weak demand or loss of key customers<br>- Large, debt-funded acquisitions/capex materially impacting the debt metrics on sustained basis; for instance, if debt/Ebitda is more than 2.75-3 times<br>- Depletion of sizeable cash surpluses | |

| About the Company<br>Established in 1962 in Chennai, Brakes India manufactures brake systems (installed capacity of 9.5 million unit per annum) and ferrous castings (126,000 tonne per annum \[TPA\]). Brakes India's subsidiary, Dunes Oman, manufactures non-machined castings with capacity of 36,000 TPA. Trichur Sundaram Santhanam & Family Pvt Ltd owns ~70% stake in Brakes India, Sundram Finance Holdings Ltd ~24% and the rest is held by other TVS group companies controlled by the Santhanam family. | |
